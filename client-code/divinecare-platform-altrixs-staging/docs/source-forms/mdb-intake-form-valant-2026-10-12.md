- Substance Use Disorder (SUD)

## Confidentiality of Records

Federal law and regulations protect the confidentiality of patient records maintained by us. Generally, we may not say to a person outside the program that a patient attends the program, or disclose any information identifying a patient, unless:
- The patient consents in writing; OR
- The disclosure is allowed by a court order; OR
- The disclosure is made to medical personnel in a medical emergency or to qualified personnel for supervision or program evaluation; OR
- The patient commits or threatens to commit a crime either at the program or against any person who works for the program; OR
- In the case of communicable disease reporting; OR
- In the case of child abuse or neglect; OR
- In the case of elder abuse or neglect; OR
- In the case of harm or injury to self or others; OR
- In the case of third party payers; OR
- An investigation relating to patient's death.
- Violation of the Federal law and regulations by a program is a crime. Suspected violations may be reported to the United States Attorney in the district where the violation occurs.

Your mental health information may be used within our organization in the following ways;
- Consultations with other service providers here
- During our clinical meetings
- For billing purposes: a diagnosis is given to your insurer for reimbursement purposes, including Maryland Health Partners, Medicare, and/or private insurance companies.

## Release of Information

Sometimes other individuals or agencies may have information that give us a more complete picture of you or lend their perceptions to what's happening. Receiving or sharing personal information about you from records with any other party will require your written consent. Should there be a need or potential benefit to sharing information with another party, we will first discuss this matter with you. If your permission is given, we will then assist you with providing written consent. We also require a Release of Information form to be in your record for your emergency contact. In the case of an emergency, we may need to inform your emergency contact of any applicable treatment you are, or have, received from here.

## Duty to Warn

In certain situations, mental health providers, or any facility that provides treatment for mental disorders, have a "Duty to Warn" if a client poses a threat to himself/herself, another individual or a group of individuals.

In most cases:
- A counselor is required to breach confidentiality if the client poses animminent threat to either himself, the counselor, or a third party.
- The necessary information must be divulged to someone who is capable of taking action to reduce the threat.
- In most cases, the person who is in danger, and law enforcement would be notified.

## Rehabilitation Assessment

Everyone entering our program will participate in an assessment process to determine the nature and the extent of the problems you are facing. The assessment process will include a Rehabilitation Assessment. Your honest answers will help us see how you view the situation and will assist us in working together with you to develop a plan that truly addresses your needs and goals. At any point if something is not clear to you, please ask about it.

## Individualized Rehabilitation Plan

We provide Person-Centered planning for our participants. This process helps the counselor and the person served identify the individual's strengths, needs, abilities, and preference for recovery so that an individual treatment plan may be developed. When developing an individual's plan, we seek to include family and professional collaboration during planning, goal setting, and throughout service delivery. Regular opportunities for individuals to discuss progress towards their goals and provide feedback on their program is an important part of our treatment services.

Person-centered planning involves the development of a "toolbox" of methods and resources that enable people to be involved in the planning process, and to take ownership of their own paths to success. Our goal is for you to meet your goals!

## Course of Treatment Services and Activities

During your treatment with us, you will be engaging in a variety of services and activities that may include but not be limited to the following:

**Outpatient Assessment** – A bio-psychosocial history including behavioral health or substance use history, laboratory testing, and other relevant measures.

**Treatment Plan development** – A course of action recommended by our team with your input to achieve your treatment goals. Activities and target dates will help you on your way.

**Individual and/or Group counseling** – most of our programs include both settings, but your treatment program will be individualized to your needs, abilities and preferences.

**Psychiatric Rehabilitation Plan** – A service recommended by our clinical team with your approval to help you with community resources and enhance living skills.

**Psychiatric Evaluation** – A service by a Psychiatric or Psychiatric Nurse Practitioner evaluating what your needs might be.

**SUD Services** – Any Clients with this need will get services in regards to PH and IOP services.

We ask that you participate fully in each activity as it will enhance its meaning to you as an individual. Our goal is ultimately to help you achieve goals that you identify as important.

## Transition / Discharge Planning

Your PRP counselor will work with you to develop a plan that will assist you to achieve the goals on your personalized plan. Transition/Discharge plans will be talked about early in your treatment; however, we recognize that it can take time to help each program participant reach their personal objectives/goals. It is our mission to help you meet your goals and target dates, and keep you informed of your progress towards completion of your program. Your Transition/Discharge Plan will help you continue your success upon discharge from our care. This plan may include strategies to continue with your treatment for your mental health and substance use disorders (if applicable). At your discretion, family members can participate in your transition/discharge planning and will be invited to attend a transition/discharge session.

## Transition / Discharge Criteria

Patients are successfully discharged when all treatment goals have been met. A patient may be transitioned to a lesser level of care if there is a social support system in place and the patient is able to follow a self-directed recovery plan without substantial risk of relapse.

Other discharge circumstances may include:

The patient need to be transferred to a higher level of care for mental health treatment and/or substance use treatment;

The patient decides to no longer participate in the program;

The patient consistently misses scheduled appointments and exhibits an overall un-investment in complying with the recommendations of the treatment team.

## Transition Criteria

A patient can be transferred to another program service when it is determined by the treatment team that the patient would benefit from a higher or lower level of care, or a different program. A Transition Plan will be created by you and your service provider. When it is deemed appropriate for a patient to be discharged from a program, either successfully or not, a discharge notification and summary is completed, sent to the patient and a copy placed in the patient's record.

## Policy Concerning Child and Adult Abuse

As a recipient of services here, you are required to be familiar with the Maryland Statues regarding child and vulnerable adult abuse. Under Maryland law any health practitioner, police officer, or human service worker who has reason to believe that a vulnerable adult is in danger is required to report that fact to the local department of social services.

Maryland Statutes protect children and vulnerable adults from abuse and/or neglect. The law assigns to DSS all responsibility for receiving, investigating and acting upon such reports.

**CODE OF MARYLAND REGULATIONS (COMAR) defines abuse and neglect as:**

Physical injury not (necessarily visible) of a child or vulnerable adult under circumstances that indicate that the individual's health or welfare is harmed or at substantial risk of being harmed.

The failure to give proper care and attention to a child or vulnerable adult, leaving a child or vulnerable adult unattended where the individual's health or welfare is harmed or a child or vulnerable adult is placed in substantial risk of harm.

An act or acts involving sexual molestation or exploitation whether physical injuries are sustained or not.

Identifiable and substantial impairment of a child or vulnerable adult or psychological ability to function.
